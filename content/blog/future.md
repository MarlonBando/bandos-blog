In Danish, the future tense doesn't exist.

In case you don't know, I have now been more than a year and a half in Denmark and I plan to stay longer. That's why I finally decided to learn Danish (or at least try).

In today's class, the professor told us that the future tense doesn't exist in the language. Of course, you can communicate that something hasn't happened yet, mixing the present tense with an auxiliary verb, similarly to English. But still, isn't it crazy that a language doesn't have a future tense? It blew my mind.

This resonated with me all day (no surprise I'm writing this post), and then my mom shared with me an Instagram post saying that Copenhagen was once again the happiest city in the world.

Is this a coincidence? The [hygge](https://denmark.dk/people-and-culture/hygge#:~:text=A%20warm%20atmosphere,life%27s%20quieter%20pleasures.) culture is the pillar of a country that doesn't have a future tense in its language.

When I was a teenager, I got influenced a lot by American hustle culture on YouTube. I don't regret it because that mindset brought me to where I am now and made me the person I am today, and I like myself, so I don't regret it. However, every time I reach a goal, it's just the start of a new one. I remember when I was graduating from high school (attention! flexing moment incoming) I aimed for the highest available grade, and after months of hard preparation I got it. I felt empty because everything I had worked for was gone and it wasn't anything special. I can give you way more examples than that, but the important thing is that all my energy is always pointed at the next thing. The moment I get there, it's already the past, and I have to look forward again just to feel alive. The result is an eternity of dissatisfaction.

That's why I don't think it is a coincidence. Of course the hygge culture is from a country that does not have the future tense in its language. Not looking forward and just enjoying the present moment and sharing that moment with people is where peace and happiness are, and during my stay in Denmark I will try to develop my hygge side as much as I can, but not too much. Chasing goals is who I am and I will keep doing it. I will just try to celebrate them a bit more.

If you made it this far, ask yourself if you are more hygge or more the American hustle way, and where you would like to be more.
